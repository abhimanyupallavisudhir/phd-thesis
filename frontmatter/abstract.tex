\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

\begin{drafttext}
This thesis develops an economic perspective on AI alignment at three levels.
First, it studies markets as architectures for agency and the relationship
between prices, rewards, learning, and rationality. Second, it treats scalable
oversight as a problem of information asymmetry between a more-informed agent
and a less-informed evaluator. Third, it studies mechanisms for eliciting and
evaluating beliefs when timely, objective ground truth is unavailable. Together,
these levels ask how economic mechanisms can construct agents, align their
incentives, and evaluate the beliefs on which their actions depend.
\end{drafttext}
