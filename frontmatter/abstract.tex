\chapter{Abstract}

\begin{draftblock}
Economics is the study of aligning selfish agents to a social goal; AI alignment is the problem of ensuring that artificial agents pursue their principals' goals. This thesis develops this analogy at three levels. First, at the level of \emph{agency}: we study market-based architectures for reinforcement learning and argue that economic notions --- prices, recursive value flows, hyperstitional information --- provide the rational theory of reward and credit assignment in general agents. Second, at the level of \emph{alignment}: we recast the outer alignment/scalable oversight problem as one of information asymmetry between a principal (the human) and more capable agents (AIs), and propose recursive information markets as a mechanism for extrapolating human volition, together with a benchmark for evaluating scalable oversight protocols. Third, at the level of \emph{elicitation}: aligning agents to human values requires eliciting beliefs on questions that are subjective, latent, or otherwise lack ground truth; we construct scoring mechanisms for claims that are neither verifiable nor falsifiable, and develop consistency-based evaluation and correction methods for language-model forecasters.
\end{draftblock}
